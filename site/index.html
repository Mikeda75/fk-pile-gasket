The Problem

Every pile jacket needs a bottom seal.
Most crews still improvise one.

When a pile encapsulation form is filled with concrete or grout, the base has to be sealed — or fill bleeds into the waterway and out of your pour. The industry's standard answer is still foam stuffed into the annular space, pleated plastic "diaper" skirts with pins and banding, or bolted clamps. All of it is fiddly, multi-part diver work.

The improvised way

  • Stuffed foam, plastic sheeting, skirt pins, zip-tie bands
  • Multiple parts assembled underwater by feel
  • More diver bottom time on every pile
  • Single-use waste, unreliable seal

The Pile Gasket way

  • One piece — zip on, cinch two drawstrings, done
  • Installs around the pile; no threading over the top
  • Locking zipper holds under load; removed after cure
  • Contains fill at the form base — less waste, cleaner water

How to Order

Measure twice, order once.

Measure the perimeter

Circumference for round piles; total perimeter for square or H-piles. Measure at the gasket elevation.

Measure the height

The coverage height you need at the base of the encapsulation form.

Request your quote

Send sizes and quantities. We confirm dimensions, price, and lead time — then build to order.

Size = Perimeter (C) × Height (H) — billed by the sq ft at $6.04, rounded up to the nearest half foot. Example: 51" × 36" ≈ 13 sq ft.
